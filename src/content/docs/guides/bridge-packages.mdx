A **bridge package** joins two packages that do not know about each other. Neither of them depends on
the other. The bridge depends on both, and holds only what makes sense when both are installed.

The bundled `scene` package is built this way. It runs scenes, and needs no other package. Its
`+help` topics and its settings reach `+help` and `+config` through two bridges, `scene-help` and
`scene-config`:

![The scene package depends on nothing. scene-help depends on scene and plus-help, and adds SRC`SCENE to the +help librarian. scene-config depends on scene and plus-config, and adds SYSTEM`SCENE to the +config registry](../../../assets/guide/bridge-packages-scene.svg)

This page covers:

- why a package is split this way;
- the scene bridges, as a worked example;
- writing a bridge;
- when a dependency is the better choice.

For the places one package lets another change its behaviour, see [Extension Points](/guides/extension-points/).

## Why bridges

A game installs the packages it wants. If `scene` depended on `plus-help`, a game could not run
scenes without also running `+help`, and removing `+help` would mean removing scenes first.

A bridge keeps each package whole on its own:

- Each package installs, upgrades and uninstalls without the other.
- No package checks at run time whether another is installed. The code that needs both is in the
  bridge, and the bridge is only there when both are.
- Uninstalling a bridge removes only the joining. The two packages are left as they were.

## The scene bridges

`scene` keeps its own data in the shape the other packages read:

- Its help topics are a `HELP` tree on its own **Scene Help** object, the way `+help` reads topics.
- Its settings are ``DATA`CONFIG`<NAME>`` attributes on the Scene Logger, with the leaves `+config`
  reads: the type, the default, the limits and a description. The scene commands read each setting
  with `get()`, whether or not `+config` is installed.

On its own, nothing outside the package reads that data. Each bridge is the one registration that
makes another package read it:

| Bridge | Depends on | What it adds |
|---|---|---|
| `scene-help` | `scene`, `plus-help` | ``SRC`SCENE`` on the `+help` librarian, naming the Scene Help object, and a line at the end of `+scene/help` |
| `scene-config` | `scene`, `plus-config` | ``SYSTEM`SCENE`` on the `+config` registry, naming the Scene Logger |

A new game installs `plus-help`, `plus-config`, `scene` and both bridges at its first start, so it
has `+help scene` and `+config scene` as before. A bundled bridge installs only where both of the
packages it joins are installed: a game that turned `scene` off does not get either bridge.

### With and without the bridges

Without `scene-help` and `scene-config`, scenes run as before, and `+help` and `+config` do not
know about them:

```text
> +help scene
[HELP] Warning: No local topic 'scene'. Try help scene or +help/search scene.
> +config scene
[CONFIG] Warning: No system is called scene. +config lists them.
```

`+scene/help` still lists every `+scene` command, because the scene package has their usage lines
itself. With `scene-help` installed, the list ends with where to read more:

```text
> +scene/help
══════════════════════════════╡ Scene commands ╞══════════════════════════════
+scene/activate <id>
+scene/as <name>, or +scene/as on its own to pose under your own name again
...
+scene/upcoming [<days>|all]

+help scene explains them.
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

Settings changed while `+config` was not installed are kept. Here a game set two of them on the
Scene Logger directly, then installed `plus-config` and `scene-config`:

```text
> &DATA`CONFIG`RECALL-ROUNDS <Scene Logger>=1
Scene Logger/DATA`CONFIG`RECALL-ROUNDS - Set.
> &DATA`CONFIG`DEFAULT-STATUS <Scene Logger>=new
Scene Logger/DATA`CONFIG`DEFAULT-STATUS - Set.
```

### The whole of scene-help

```yaml
format: "1.10"
package: scene-help
version: 1.0.0
description: "Puts the scene topics into +help: registers the scene package's help with the +help librarian, and +scene/help points at +help scene."
category: Scenes

depends:
  - package: scene
    version: ">=1.12"
    source:
      repo: https://github.com/SharpMUSH/SharpMUSH
      path: examples/packages/scene/
      branch: main
  - package: plus-help
    version: ">=1.0"
    source:
      repo: https://github.com/SharpMUSH/SharpMUSH
      path: examples/packages/plus-help/
      branch: main

objects:
  - ref: help_registration
    target: "{{plus-help/librarian}}"
    attributes:
      SRC`SCENE: |-
        {{scene/scene_help}}

  - ref: help_pointer
    target: "{{scene/logger}}"
    attributes:
      FILTER`HELP`PLUSHELP: |-
        %0%r%r+help scene explains them.
```

It creates no object. Both entries are attach entries: they add attributes to objects the other two
packages created, and uninstalling `scene-help` removes those attributes again. The second entry
uses the scene package's `HELP` [filter point](/guides/extension-points/#filter-points).

Packages that add scene topics, such as `scene-verse` and `radio-scene`, attach them to the Scene
Help object. They reach `+help` through the same registration, and need no bridge of their own.

## Writing a bridge

1. **Keep your data where it belongs.** The package keeps its own data on its own objects, in the
   shape the other package reads: a `HELP` tree for `+help`, ``DATA`CONFIG`` leaves for `+config`.
   Data nobody reads does no harm, and it is already there when the bridge arrives.
2. **Put only the joining in the bridge.** A registration leaf, a filter, an event attribute, a line
   on another package's card. If the bridge grows commands of its own, it may be a package in its
   own right.
3. **Depend on both.** `depends:` on each package makes their objects legal attach targets, such as
   `{{plus-help/librarian}}` and `{{scene/logger}}`, and keeps either from being uninstalled while
   the bridge is attached to it.
4. **Name it after both.** `<package>-<other>`, as `scene-help`, `radio-scene` and `roster-jobs` are.
   Give it the category of the package it extends.
5. **Read the other package's data, not its code.** When the bridge must call another object's code,
   use `@trigger/inline/nobreak/args`, not `@include`: a `{{ref}}` in the included code is looked
   up on the object running it, which is the bridge's caller, not the bridge.
6. **Nothing assumes the bridge.** The package's own messages never send a player to a command from
   the other package. `+scene` says `+scene/help lists the commands`, and the bridge adds the line
   about `+help scene`.

Changing a bridge bumps its version, as with any package. When the package it extends changes the
data a bridge reads, raise the bridge's `version:` range on it.

<Aside>
A bridge is a package like any other, so a game can uninstall one to keep the two packages apart.
Uninstalling `scene-config` leaves the scene settings on the Scene Logger, where staff can still set
them with `&`.
</Aside>

## Bridge or dependency

Depend on a package when yours cannot do its job without it: `relationships-app` draws the
relationships package's data and has nothing to show without it.

Use a bridge when the other package adds an ability on top: a help topic, a setting screen, a
line on a card, a scene log for the radio. Signs that a package wants a bridge:

- It depends on another package only to attach one registration leaf to it.
- It draws a section about another system, such as a room display that builds a scene block itself.
  The section belongs in a bridge that fills a point the display offers.
- Most of its commands are not about the other package. An availability calendar is mostly about
  when characters are free; showing who in a scene's cast is free is the part that needs scenes.
- It reads another package's fields for one line of output, such as a card line drawn from a
  profile's fields.

The bundled bridges:

| Bridge | Joins | What it adds |
|---|---|---|
| `scene-help` | `scene`, `plus-help` | The scene topics in `+help` |
| `scene-config` | `scene`, `plus-config` | The scene settings in `+config` |
| `radio-scene` | `radio`, `scene` | `+radio/log`: what a listener hears goes into their scene |
| `relationships-scene` | `relationships`, `scene` | Last scene together on the `+rel` card and the name card |
| `roster-jobs` | `roster`, `jobs` | Roster applications through the job queue |
| `health-jobs` | `health`, `jobs` | Staff load in `+health` |
