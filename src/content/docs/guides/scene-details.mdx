A scene has a title and a pitch. Players often want to say more: which **verse** or setting it takes
place in, which **canon** it draws on, whether it carries a **content warning**, which era it is set
in. These are a scene's **details**. The scene's owner sets them, they show on the scene's card, at
the top of its log and on its page in the portal, and players can list scenes by them.

The `scene` package keeps any detail an owner names. A game can also list the details it uses, with a
name and a hint for each. The `scene-verse` package lists three, a verse, a canon and a content
warning, and shows them more prominently.

A single pose can carry something of its own too, such as being a flashback. That is a **mark**,
covered further down.

## Setting a scene's details

`+scene/set <detail>=<value>` sets a detail on your focused scene. The detail is any word you choose:

```text
> +scene/create Salt Market at Dusk
[SCENE] Done: Scene 1 created and focused (status: active).
> +scene/set verse=Marvel
[SCENE] Done: Scene 1: Verse is now Marvel.
> +scene/set canon=Marvel|X-Men
[SCENE] Done: Scene 1: Canon is now Marvel · X-Men.
> +scene/set era=1980s
[SCENE] Done: Scene 1: Era is now 1980s.
```

- `+scene/set <id>/<detail>=<value>` sets one on a scene you own without focusing it.
- `+scene/set <detail>=` with nothing after the `=` clears it.
- `|` splits several values.

Only the scene's owner, or staff, can set its details, as with its title and pitch. A detail's name is
a word of letters, digits, `-` and `_`, and it can't be one of the things a scene already has:

```text
> +scene/set title=Oops
[SCENE] Warning: 'title' is a scene field already, so it can't be a detail
```

Use `+scene/title` and `+scene/pitch` for those.

`+scene/details` says which details your game lists. With only the `scene` package installed, it
lists none:

```text
> +scene/details
[SCENE] Warning: This game lists no scene details. You can still set your own: +scene/set <name>=<value>.
```

### Where details show

The scene's card, from `+scene <id>`, has a Details section between the pitch and the players. Details
the game does not list go by name:

```text
> +scene 1
=======================< Scene 1: Salt Market at Dusk >=======================
Status:    active
Owner:     Ilsa
Where:     Lower Docks
Created:   Oct 14 11:06PM
Scheduled: -
Watchers:  Anyone
===================================< Pitch >==================================
Lanterns, rumours and a missing ledger.
==================================< Details >=================================
Canon  Marvel · X-Men
Era    1980s
Verse  Marvel
==================================< Players >=================================
Status  Name                                                             Poses
Owner   Ilsa                                                                 1
==============================================================================
```

`+scene/log` puts every detail on the line under the scene's title:

```text
> +scene/log
=< Scene 1: Salt Market at Dusk >=============================================
Canon: Marvel · X-Men · Era: 1980s · Verse: Marvel
=< Ilsa · pose 1 >================================================< Scene 1 >=
Ilsa sets her lantern down on the crate.
```

In the portal, the scene's page lists them under Details beside the log. Each value there is a link
to every scene that gives the detail the same value.

### Finding scenes by detail

Give a detail and a value after `+scene/list`, `+scene/old` or `+scene/mine`. Case does not matter,
and `*` matches anything, so `canon=*x-men*` finds every scene whose canon mentions X-Men:

```text
> +scene/list canon=*x-men*
======================< Scenes: active · canon=*x-men* >======================
ID  Title                                            Where        Cast  Status
 1  Salt Market at Dusk                              Lower Docks     1  active
==============================================================================
1 scene
```

A page number goes before the detail: `+scene/old/2 verse=Marvel`. Bare `+scene` takes no detail,
because `+scene <id>` shows a scene's card; use `+scene/list` instead.

In the portal, the search box on a character's Scenes page matches details as well as titles, pitches
and rooms. On the scene list, clicking a detail's value shows only the scenes with that value, such as
every scene whose verse is Marvel. A line over the list says what it is narrowed to, with a button back
to every scene. The address is `/scenes?detail=verse&value=Marvel`, so it can be shared.

The scene list's sidebar has a section for each detail the game lists: the values scenes give it, the
ones most scenes give first, with how many scenes give each. A value is counted once per scene, and a
scene you can't open is not counted.

### On the portal's scene cards

A scene's card in the portal's scene list shows the details the game lists (`listed`) on a line under
its pitch, each with its name: `Content warning Violence, Blood   Verse Marvel`. They come in the
game's order. A detail with a [tone](#listing-details) shows its values in that theme colour, so a
content warning stands out. The line over the card keeps only the scene's state: live, paused or
private.

## Verse, canon and content warnings

The `scene-verse` package comes with SharpMUSH but is not installed on a new game. Install it from
the admin panel's Packages page. It lists three details:

| Detail | Name | What goes in it |
|---|---|---|
| `warning` | Content warning | What a reader may want to know before joining; several are allowed |
| `verse` | Verse | The setting or universe the scene takes place in |
| `canon` | Canon | The source works the scene draws on; several are allowed |

```text
> +scene/details
===============================< Scene details >==============================
Detail   Name             What goes in it         Takes
warning  Content warning  What a reader may want  any text, several split by |
                          to know before
                          joining, such as
                          violence. Split
                          several with |.
verse    Verse            The setting or          any text
                          universe the scene
                          takes place in.
canon    Canon            The source works the    any text, several split by |
                          scene draws on. Split
                          several with |.
==============================================================================
You can set other details too: +scene/set <name>=<value>.
```

Players still set any other detail they like. With the package installed:

- The content warning and the verse follow the title in `+scene`, `+scene/old` and `+scene/mine`, and
  show on the portal's scene cards.
- The three show first on the card, in the order of the table above, so the content warning is read
  first. Its tone is `warning`, so it is in the reader's warning colour wherever it appears.
- Whoever joins a scene with a content warning is told it as they join.
- `+scene/verses` lists the verses of the scenes running now.

```text
> +scene/set warning=Violence
[SCENE] Done: Scene 1: Content warning is now Violence.
> +scene/list
==============================< Scenes: active >==============================
ID  Title                                            Where        Cast  Status
 1  Salt Market at Dusk · Violence · Marvel          Lower Docks     1  active
==============================================================================
1 scene
```

Another player joining sees the warning:

```text
> +scene/join 1
[SCENE] Done: Joined and focused on scene 1.
[SCENE] Content warning: Violence
> +scene/verses
=============================< Verses played now >============================
Verse                                                                   Scenes
Marvel                                                                       1
==============================================================================
+scene/list verse=<verse> lists a verse's scenes.
```

Uninstalling the package stops listing the three. Scenes keep the values they were given, and those
then show by name, like any other detail.

### In the portal

The scene's owner sets details from the **Edit** button on the scene's page. Each detail the game
lists has its own field, with its hint underneath. A detail with choices is a drop-down, and one that
takes several lets the owner tick more than one. Saving runs `+scene/set` as the owner's character, so
a value the game refuses is refused here too, with the same message.

## Marking a pose

Some poses are not like the rest of the scene: a flashback, a dream, a vision. `+scene/mark` gives one
pose a mark, and `+scene/unmark` takes it off. The pose's author or the scene's owner can mark it.

```text
> pose sets her lantern down on the crate.
=< Ilsa · pose 1 >================================================< Scene 1 >=
Ilsa sets her lantern down on the crate.
> +scene/mark 1=flashback
[SCENE] Done: Pose 1 marked flashback.
> +scene/recall 1=1
=< Ilsa · pose 1 >====================================< Scene 1 · flashback >=
Ilsa sets her lantern down on the crate.
> +scene/unmark 1=flashback
[SCENE] Done: Pose 1 is no longer marked flashback.
```

A mark is one word. A pose can have several. In the portal, the scene's page offers a chip for each
mark used in the scene, and picking one shows only the poses that have it.

## Listing details

Staff holding the `layout.admin` permission decide which details the game lists. Each is one
attribute on the Scene Logger, `` DETAIL`<KEY> ``, holding JSON:

| Field | What it does |
|---|---|
| `label` | Its name, as players read it |
| `hint` | One line saying what goes in it, shown by `+scene/details` and under the portal's field |
| `choices` | The values it allows; without it, any text |
| `many` | `true` when a scene may give several values, split by `\|` |
| `listed` | `true` to show it after the title in lists, on the portal's scene cards and in the scene list's sidebar |
| `tone` | The theme colour its values show in, in `+scene` and the portal; without it, the text's own colour |
| `order` | Its place, lowest first |

The tone is one of the theme colours `tone()` takes, as for [pose types](/guides/scene-pose-types#in-the-portal):
`foreground`, `primary`, `secondary`, `tertiary`, `muted`, `success`, `warning`, `error` or `info`.

The commands write the JSON for you and refuse anything that would not read:

- `+scene/detail/add <detail>=<name>` lists a new one.
- `+scene/detail/set <detail>/<field>=<value>` changes one field. `choices` takes the values split
  by `|`; `many` and `listed` take `yes` or `no`; `tone` takes a theme colour, or nothing to clear it.
- `+scene/detail <detail>` shows one in full.
- `+scene/detail/remove <detail>` stops listing it. Scenes keep the values they had.

For example, a game with a fixed set of moods:

```text
> +scene/detail/add mood=Mood
[SCENE] Done: Added scene detail mood. +scene/detail/set mood/<field>=<value> changes it.
> +scene/detail/set mood/choices=Light|Tense|Dark
[SCENE] Done: Scene detail mood: choices set.
> +scene/detail/set mood/listed=yes
[SCENE] Done: Scene detail mood: listed set.
> +scene/detail/set mood/hint=How the scene feels.
[SCENE] Done: Scene detail mood: hint set.
> +scene/detail mood
=============================< Scene detail mood >============================
Detail:          mood
Name:            Mood
What goes in it: How the scene feels.
Takes:           Light, Tense, or Dark
In lists:        yes
Tone:            -
Order:           50
==============================================================================
```

A player who gives a value that is not a choice is told which ones there are. One that differs only
in case is kept the way the choice is written:

```text
> +scene/set mood=gloomy
[SCENE] Warning: Mood can be Light, Tense, Dark
> +scene/set mood=tense
[SCENE] Done: Scene 1: Mood is now Tense.
```

The `open-details` setting decides whether players may set details the game does not list. It is
`yes` as shipped. Turn it off to keep them to the list (see
[Game Settings with +config](/guides/config)):

```
> +config scene/open-details=no
[CONFIG] Done: scene/open-details is now no, was yes.
```

## Changing what +scene shows

A package can change what `+scene` shows without editing the `scene` package. It adds a **filter** to
the Scene Logger: an attribute named `` FILTER`<point>`<name> ``. Where `+scene` draws that point, it
passes what it would show through every filter on the point with `chain()`, in name order. Each filter
gets what would be shown as `%0` and returns what is shown instead, and the next filter gets that.

| Point | `%0` | Also given |
|---|---|---|
| `CARD` | The body of the card from `+scene <id>`, between its title and its last rule | `%1` the scene |
| `` LIST`TITLE `` | A scene's title in `+scene`, `+scene/old` and `+scene/mine`, with its listed details | `%1` the scene |
| `` LOG`HEAD `` | The lines over `+scene/log` | `%1` the scene |
| `` POSE`RULE `` | The right side of the line over a pose, as plain text | `%1` the scene, `%2` the pose |
| `` DETAIL`SHOW `` | A scene's value for a detail, everywhere it is shown | `%1` the scene, `%2` the detail |
| `JOIN` | What a player is told when they join | `%1` the scene, `%2` the player |

A filter runs as the Scene Logger, with `%#` the player reading. For example, these two filters show
every verse in capitals and put a scene's era at the top of its card:

```text
&FILTER`DETAIL`SHOW`CAPS <Scene Logger>=if(strmatch(%2,verse),ucstr(%0),%0)
&FILTER`CARD`ERA <Scene Logger>=if(strlen(scene(%1,era)),Set in the [scene(%1,era)].%r)%0
```

```text
> +scene/set verse=Marvel
[SCENE] Done: Scene 1: Verse is now MARVEL.
> +scene 1
=======================< Scene 1: Salt Market at Dusk >=======================
Set in the 1980s.
Status:    active
Owner:     Ilsa
Where:     Lower Docks
Created:   Oct 14 11:06PM
Scheduled: -
Watchers:  Anyone
===================================< Pitch >==================================
Lanterns, rumours and a missing ledger.
==================================< Details >=================================
Content warning  Violence
Verse            MARVEL
Canon            Marvel · X-Men
Mood             Tense
Era              1980s
==================================< Players >=================================
Status       Name                                                        Poses
Owner        Ilsa                                                            1
Participant  Ann                                                             0
==============================================================================
```

The `scene-verse` package works this way. Its content warning takes its colour from its `tone`, and
one filter tells a joiner about it:

```yaml
objects:
  - ref: verse
    target: "{{scene/logger}}"
    attributes:
      DETAIL`WARNING: |-
        {"label":"Content warning","hint":"What a reader may want to know before joining, such as violence. Split several with |.","many":true,"listed":true,"tone":"warning","order":5}
      FILTER`JOIN`VERSE: |-
        %0[if(strlen(scene(%1,warning)),%r[notice(SCENE,Content warning: [u(%!/FUN`DETAIL`SHOW,%1,warning)])])]
```

Because the package attaches its attributes to the `scene` package's Scene Logger, uninstalling it
takes its filters and its `DETAIL` attributes away again. See [Managing attributes on an object you didn't create](/reference/package-format#managing-attributes-on-an-object-you-didnt-create)
for how attaching works.

<Aside>
Name a filter after your package, as `` FILTER`JOIN`VERSE `` is, so two packages never write the same
attribute. Filters on one point run in the order of their names.
</Aside>

## In softcode

A detail is a key of the scene, so `scene()` reads it. A listed detail reads empty until it is set.

```text
> think scene(1,verse)
Marvel
> think scene(1,details)
canon era verse
```

| Function | Returns |
|---|---|
| `scene(<id>,<detail>)` | The scene's value for a detail; several are split by `\|` |
| `scene(<id>,details)` | The details the scene has a value for: the listed ones in order, then the rest by name |
| `scenedetails()` | The details the game lists, in order |
| `scenedetail(<detail>[,<field>])` | One field of a listed detail: `label` (the default), `hint`, `choices`, `many`, `listed`, `tone` or `order` |
| `scenedetailvalue(<detail>,<value>)` | The value as the detail would keep it, or `#-1` and why it would be refused |
| `scenedetailcheck(<detail>,<json>)` | Empty when the JSON reads as a detail, or `#-1` and why not |

A pose's marks are its tags: `scenepose(<scene>,<pose>,tags)`.

Writing a detail from softcode goes through `@scene/set <id>/<detail>=<value>`, which only the Scene
Logger and wizards can run. Players use `+scene/set`, which checks that they own the scene first.
